# AION Wonders — Why Doesn't a Solar Eclipse Happen Every Month?

Status: creative preview only — not scheduled or published

## Fact-first brief

- Question: The Moon reaches a new-moon phase each month, so why does its shadow not cross Earth every month?
- Reality anchor: A solar eclipse requires the Moon to pass between the Sun and Earth, casting a shadow on Earth.
- Mechanism: The Moon's orbit is tilted by about five degrees relative to the Earth-Sun plane. At most new moons its shadow passes above or below Earth. Eclipses become possible only during eclipse seasons, when a new moon crosses the Earth-Sun plane.
- Supported conclusion: A new moon alone is not enough; alignment and the Moon's shadow path must also be right.
- Creative boundary: The sky may be cinematic and AION may guide the eye, but no aurora, crystal, or decorative light is allowed to substitute for the Sun-Moon-Earth geometry.

## Sources

1. [NASA: Why Do Eclipses Happen?](https://science.nasa.gov/eclipses/geometry/) — solar eclipses occur when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth; the tilted lunar orbit and eclipse seasons explain why this does not happen each month.
2. [NASA Space Place: How Is the Sun Completely Blocked in an Eclipse?](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/total-solar-eclipse/en/) — the Moon and Sun can appear nearly the same size because of their relative distance and size.
