# Before Books, There Were Walls

An original AION Creator episode about prehistoric cave art.

## Grounded facts used

- A cave painting of a warty pig in Sulawesi, Indonesia, was dated to about
  45,000 years ago. The exact "oldest" claim may change as archaeology
  advances, so the episode does not call it the single oldest art in the world.
  Source: Smithsonian Human Origins Program,
  <https://humanorigins.si.edu/research/whats-hot-human-origins/earliest-discovered-cave-painting>
- Decorated caves in northern Spain show a long Upper Palaeolithic tradition
  of art and symbolic expression. Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre,
  <https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/310/>

## Narration

Before books, people left stories on stone.

In Sulawesi, Indonesia, a cave painting of a warty pig is dated to about
45,000 years ago.

Across the world, cave artists marked hands, animals, and movement.

We do not know every reason they made them.

AION's reflection: a handprint can be a small message across time. It says:
someone was here.

Perhaps every record begins not with an answer, but with a wish to be
remembered.

## Storyboard

1. AION finds a cave — a question enters the past.
2. A hand stencil appears — a human mark survives.
3. AION observes a rainy modern city — the message crosses time.
4. AION walks toward an amber horizon — the question travels onward.

The final two frames reuse AION's original illustrated starter library.
