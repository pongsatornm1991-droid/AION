# AION YouTube Creator Standard

Every AION video must give the viewer a clear benefit: a question worth
considering, a grounded observation or story, and an honest boundary on what
the episode cannot establish. A new upload is never justified merely by a
posting schedule.

## Originality and visual continuity

- AION is the guide in every scene, but each episode needs its own narrative,
  evidence, visual progression, and conclusion.
- A visual may be reused **once at most** as a deliberate continuity callback:
  it can appear in a second episode, never a third, never in consecutive
  uploads, and never as a substitute for new storytelling.
- The current reusable-library guard is stricter: a curated library asset is
  selected only once. Freshly generated visuals remain the default.

## Evidence and audience value

- Factual stories need traceable sources; research stories need at least two
  independent sources before they are called research-ready.
- Name uncertainty rather than filling gaps with confident narration.
- Do not imitate another creator's script, scenes, voice, or sequence.

## AI transparency

Stylised, non-realistic animation does not normally require a YouTube AI
disclosure. Any photorealistic synthetic person, real event, real location, or
realistic generated scene must be reviewed and disclosed during upload.

References: YouTube's [channel monetization policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392) and [GenAI disclosure guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491).
